## **Lead Intelligence & Marketplace Platform**

**Discover → Verify → Qualify → Sell → Learn → Improve**

The Core

> **Find the right prospects → verify them → score them → package them → sell them.**

Your internal acquisition machinery can be sophisticated, but the customer-facing product should be dramatically simpler.

## The product should have two sides

```text
                    LEAD GENERATION PLATFORM
                              │
             ┌────────────────┴────────────────┐
             │                                 │
      SUPPLY / OPERATIONS                 CUSTOMER / BUYER
             │                                 │
       Find companies                    Browse leads
       Find contacts                     Search/filter
       Enrich data                       Preview
       Verify                            Purchase
       Score                             Subscribe
       Monitor                           Export/API
       Package                           Track usage
```

That distinction is important.

You don't want your customers seeing your internal machinery. They want **good leads**, not a tour of your ETL pipeline.

# 4. Don't sell "contact information"

Sell **qualified opportunities**.

This distinction matters enormously.

Instead of:

> "1,000 emails for $500"

your product should communicate:

> **1,000 verified decision-makers matching your ICP**

And ideally:

> **1,000 verified CTOs at US SaaS companies with 50–500 employees, recently showing relevant buying signals.**

That makes your data defensible as a premium product.

# 6. You should introduce "Lead Quality"

This could become one of your strongest product concepts.

For example:

### Verified

Email and company information verified.

### Qualified

Matches the buyer's target criteria.

### High Intent

Additional signals indicate potential purchasing activity.

### Sales Ready

Meets your highest qualification threshold.

Then:

```text
96
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
SALES READY
```

You're essentially selling **confidence**.

# 9. Pricing model

I'd consider **three layers**.

### Pay per lead

Simple.

```text
Verified Lead        $0.50
Qualified Lead       $1.00
High Intent Lead     $2.00
Sales Ready          $3.00+
```

Obviously the actual prices depend heavily on the vertical and exclusivity.

### Lead credits

```text
100 credits     $99
500 credits     $399
2,000 credits   $1,299
```

### Subscription

```text
Starter
1,000 leads/month

Growth
5,000 leads/month

Enterprise
Custom volume
API
Dedicated datasets
Exclusive leads
```

I would strongly consider **credits**, because they give you flexibility across different lead categories.

# 10. Exclusivity could become a premium product

This is where the business gets interesting.

You could offer:

### Shared

Lead can be sold to multiple customers.

### Limited

Lead sold to a small number of customers.

### Exclusive

Lead sold to one customer.

For example:

```text
Standard Lead
$0.80

Premium Lead
$2.00

Exclusive Lead
$8.00
```

Obviously, exclusivity needs careful contractual and operational rules, but it creates a much stronger premium tier.

---

# 11. Lead freshness should be visible

This is critical.

A lead database becomes garbage remarkably quickly.

Show:

```text
Verified 2 days ago
```

rather than hiding it.

I'd make freshness part of the product:

```text
Freshness

🟢 < 7 days
🟢 < 30 days
🟡 30–90 days
🔴 > 90 days
```

And allow customers to filter:

> **Only show leads verified within the last 30 days.**

That is a valuable differentiator.

---

# 12. Lead replacement guarantee

This could be one of your strongest commercial promises.

For example:

> **If a purchased lead fails verification, we replace it.**

Then your customer isn't buying a CSV.

They're buying **validated data with accountability**.

Your backend needs to track:

```text
Lead
 ↓
Purchased
 ↓
Delivered
 ↓
Customer validation
 ↓
Accepted / Rejected
 ↓
Replacement if eligible
```

That feedback loop becomes extremely valuable.

# 16. The most important architectural concept

I'd separate your system into:

```text
                 DATA FACTORY
                      │
       ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐
       │              │              │
   Discovery      Enrichment     Validation
       │              │              │
       └──────────────┼──────────────┘
                      ↓
                 LEAD GRAPH
                      ↓
                  QUALIFIER
                      ↓
              ┌───────┴────────┐
              │                │
         MARKETPLACE           API
              │                │
              └───────┬────────┘
                      ↓
                  CUSTOMERS
                      ↓
                QUALITY FEEDBACK
                      │
                      └──────────→ DATA FACTORY
```

**The Lead Graph is your real asset.**

Not the UI.

Not the scraper.

Not the CRM.

Not even the raw database.

The asset is the continually improving relationship between:

**companies + people + attributes + signals + verification + history + buyer feedback.**
